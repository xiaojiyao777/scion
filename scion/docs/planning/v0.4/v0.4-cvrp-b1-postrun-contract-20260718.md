## Comparison fields

For every fixed view and profile, report at least:

- BKS gap, route/fleet validity, and total distance;
- lexicographic matched wins/losses/ties against canonical;
- ALNS iterations and attempts per second;
- initial/embedded VNS phase time and residual ALNS time;
- best-update count/density and accepted/improving moves;
- SA/acceptance trajectory summaries that already exist in raw rows;
- case, size, seed, execution-position, and exposure-regime heterogeneity.

Profile comparison is paired by case and seed. The report must distinguish
quality from throughput. SA cooling is iteration-based, so pure/disabled
profiles change both VNS allocation and the ALNS iteration/temperature path;
the result cannot attribute every quality difference solely to VNS wall time.

### Fixed estimands and direction

The primary statistical unit is the case, as required by v3 section 8.2. For
each view and each non-canonical profile, the closer performs this fixed
aggregation:

1. pair canonical and the profile on every retained common `(case, seed)`;
2. record the seed-level lexicographic quality outcome under the exact CVRP
   problem objective: minimize `fleet_violation` with tolerance `0.0`, then
   minimize `total_distance` with tolerance `0.001`; route count and BKS route
   count are validity/diagnostic fields, not an additional objective; record the signed
   `profile - canonical` total-distance and BKS-gap deltas, so a positive delta
   means canonical is better;
3. within each case, aggregate the seed outcomes by strict majority, with an
   equal count recorded as a tie, and take the median of each signed delta;
4. across cases, weight every retained case equally and report case wins,
   losses, ties, win rate, and the median case-level distance and gap deltas.

Seed/quartet-pooled counts and distributions are diagnostics only and cannot
set the verdict. A contrast's fixed quality direction is determined first by
the sign of `case_canonical_better - case_profile_better`; if that difference
is zero, the sign of the median case-level total-distance delta breaks the
tie; an exactly zero median remains `tie`. The direction enum is therefore
`canonical_better`, `profile_better`, or `tie`.

The two main contrasts are canonical versus pure ALNS and canonical versus
embedded-VNS-disabled. Canonical versus initial-VNS-disabled is required but
descriptive: it records whether initialization is heterogeneous, but cannot
change B1 acceptance or F1 unlock. Throughput, iterations, phase allocation,
and temperature-path measures are explanatory diagnostics and are never
quality gates.

## Acceptance rule

The closer terminates with exactly one of these operational classifications:

- `integrity_reject`: any input, identity, or integrity-closure predicate
  fails. The CLI returns a structured nonzero terminal result with this
  classification and `f1_unlocked=false`; it publishes no comparison report or
  receipt because corrupted evidence cannot authorize a final artifact. No
  comparison and no F1 unlock is allowed.
- `accepted_conservative_scope`: integrity is green; `full_256`,
  `normal_priority_boundary_excluded_248`, and `conservative_clean_212` have
  the same fixed quality direction for each of the two main contrasts; and the
  balanced 32-row view has neither a failure cliff nor the opposite direction
  from `full_256` for either main contrast. This is the only B1 verdict that
  unlocks F1.
- `diagnostic_subset_only`: integrity is green, but at least one main contrast
  changes fixed quality direction across the three main sensitivity views, or
  the balanced 32-row view reverses a main contrast. The report may describe
  unaffected matched subsets, but B1 remains diagnostic and F1 stays locked
  pending an explicit design decision.

Only the two integrity-green classifications are embedded in final report and
receipt artifacts. `integrity_reject` is deliberately unreachable from an
accepted receipt: its durable evidence is the unchanged sealed root plus the
structured nonzero closer result.

For this rule, a failure cliff means that any row in the balanced 32-row view
has non-completed status, nonzero return code, missing solver output,
infeasibility, nonzero fleet violation, or a stop reason other than the
preregistered internal `time_limit`. The expected time-limit stop itself is
not a failure. A reversed interaction means the 32-row fixed quality direction
is `canonical_better` while the corresponding full direction is
`profile_better`, or vice versa; `tie` is a sensitivity difference but not a
reversal. Absolute throughput during host overlap is marked potentially biased
and never changes the verdict.

The report is not accepted until independent integrity/code and
science/overlap reviews each report P0=0 and P1=0. No verdict triggers an
automatic rerun. Even `accepted_conservative_scope` does not prove a
production solver improvement, authorize promotion, or choose a B2 research
profile.

## Artifacts and replay

The problem-owned closer emits canonical:

- `cvrp_b1_comparison_report.v1.json`;
- `cvrp_b1_comparison_receipt.v1.json`.

The receipt binds this design SHA, input artifact SHAs, all raw identities,
overlap classification, view membership, report raw SHA, closer source SHA,
and acceptance verdict. `--check-existing` rebuilds the report from the sealed
B1 root and byte-compares report and receipt.

Publication is report first and receipt second, each with no-replace creation,
file `fsync`, and parent-directory `fsync`. If the report already exists while
the receipt does not, replay must first rebuild and byte-compare the complete
report; only an exact match permits no-replace publication of the rebuilt
receipt. A partial or mismatching report fails closed and is never overwritten.
A receipt without its report also fails closed. When both exist,
`--check-existing` validates both byte-for-byte. The closer never mutates the
experiment root outside those first-time or precisely resumed final-artifact
publications.
